Overall Implications

Demand around 'supplement' in the US is led by named nutrients rather than by health goals, with the largest pockets sitting on form and dose decisions that have already narrowed to a product choice. Goal-led searching is where the ingredient is still undecided and where a brand can still influence the outcome, while comparison and safety questions sit as a layer on top of both. A meaningful share of what looks like category demand is not the category at all, but the insurance, benefits and geometry senses of the same word.

Key Insights3
The form modifier, not the nutrient, is the real decision
Searches repeatedly specify a chemical form, a pairing or a delivery format rather than stopping at the nutrient name, which means the shopper has already accepted the ingredient and is choosing between versions. Content that resolves form-versus-form questions meets the decision where it actually happens.
Goal-led searching is the only stage still open to persuasion
When people search from a symptom or life stage instead of a nutrient, they have not chosen an ingredient yet, so the brand that translates the problem into a candidate ingredient shapes everything downstream. Once the search collapses into a bare nutrient term, the contest turns into price and availability.
Part of the head term's demand belongs to other industries entirely
Insurance, payroll, public benefit, education and geometry senses all share the word, and they arrive with no health intent at all. Any measurement or bidding built on the unqualified head term is counting an audience the brand can never serve.
What to do now3
Build form-comparison pages for the biggest nutrient families
Publish direct comparisons for the forms people name in search — magnesium glycinate against citrate and threonate, D3 with and without K2, gentler iron forms against standard ferrous sulfate — and end each with a specific product recommendation rather than a summary.
Add the non-nutrition senses as negative keywords
Exclude the insurance, Medicare, wage, benefit, education and geometry variants from paid search and site search, and restate any category volume benchmark with those senses removed so planning is based on the addressable market.
Answer the tolerability question where switching happens
People searching side effects and pregnancy-safe options are actively looking for a gentler alternative. Position gentle-form products directly against the discomfort being described, with the life-stage guidance stated plainly on the product page.
Opportunities to watch3
Claim the newer longevity and adaptogen ingredients before a brand does
The newer molecules in the data carry real demand but almost no brand attachment, which leaves the naming of the sub-category open. Early definitional and dosing content is unusually cheap to win here compared with established nutrients.
Segment the shortlist instead of publishing one ranking
Best-of searches increasingly carry a qualifier for who the product is for, so rankings built by life stage, sex or dietary pattern will fit the query better than a single universal list and are harder for a generic roundup to displace.
Treat pet supplements as an adjacent category, not a footnote
Joint and digestive supplement demand for dogs appears alongside human category terms with its own commercial weight, and it is served by a different set of publishers. It deserves separate content rather than a paragraph inside a human product page.

Overall Implications

The journey from the category term forks straight away into a short, decisive retail route and a long, meandering dictionary route, and the second one carries far more of the traffic. Most of that lexical corridor never reaches a product, and the points where it does rejoin commerce are narrow and identifiable. A third strand leaves the category entirely for insurance and namesake meanings and does not come back.

Key Insights3
The dictionary is the front door, and nobody in the category owns it
The busiest fork in the graph is a definition lookup, not a product query, and the branches leading out of it run into spelling, grammar and usage rather than shopping. Whoever answers the definitional question with a route into products captures a stream that currently dissipates.
Retail routes are short because the decision is already made
Journeys that head for a store reach a named banner within a couple of steps and then spend the rest of their length on logistics — hours, distance, directions. Nothing on that route is persuadable, so execution on listings and stock is what decides it.
Reference-list journeys are the bridge between the two halves
The route through lists of supplements starts as curiosity and ends at a question about what a person should personally take daily. It is the only common path where an informational visitor arrives at a shoppable decision under their own momentum.
What to do now3
Rewrite the definition page as a routing page
Make the nutrition meaning unmistakable in the title and opening line so the insurance and grammar branches do not absorb the click, then finish the page with a concrete next step into choosing an ingredient or format.
Fix the locator layer before the messaging layer
Store hours, distance and product-level stock decide the retail routes. Audit local listings and in-store availability feeds, and bid defensively on the generic nearby-store searches that sit one step downstream of the named banners.
Publish the daily-routine answer
Replace or supplement alphabetical indexes with a goal-based shortlist of what to take daily, organised by life stage, with a product attached to each recommendation.
Opportunities to watch3
Watch the online-store branch as an entry point
The lexical corridor now ends with people looking for places to buy vitamins online, including cheapest-place and best-site framings. That branch is an opening for a direct brand to intercept demand that started as a language question.
Separate the ambiguous senses in measurement
Reporting that treats every journey through the head term as category interest will keep overstating reach. Splitting the insurance and namesake strands out of journey reporting gives a cleaner read on genuine category momentum.
Build for the multilingual and learner entries
Journeys arrive through translation and language-learning routes as well as commercial ones. Spanish-language landing content aimed at those entries is a low-competition way to widen the top of the journey.
