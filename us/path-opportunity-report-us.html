Background & purpose

This report analyzes the consumer search journey that begins at 'supplement' in order to understand how customers move through the United States (Google) market and where they branch, drop off, or convert. Journey data from ListeningMind PathFinder was used to collect keyword-to-keyword transitions, and the key journeys and branch points (Hubs) were derived from search volume.

In the US, supplements splits at the very first step into two different people: one who wants a store to walk into, and one who wants to know what the word means. The retail branch runs straight to named chains and hours; the language branch has to travel a long way through definitions and lists before it ever reaches what to actually take, and much of it leaks into dictionary, insurance and crossword questions that never come back to the category.

Overall implications

The US supplement journey is not one funnel but two that barely touch. One is retail and local: it moves from the category straight to a named chain, a nearest store and opening hours, and it converts fast without ever asking what to take. The other is linguistic and educational: it wanders through definitions, synonyms and lists, and only reaches a product decision if something along the way turns the vocabulary question into a shortlist. Between them sit the safety and brand-trust branches, which decide whether the educational journey converts at all.

Key insights3
Retail brands, not product brands, absorb local demand
As soon as a shopper adds a location to the category, the journey jumps to chain names and then to branch, hours and directions queries. The decision being made at that moment is where to go, not what to buy, so the shelf is won through the retailer's surfaces rather than through brand search.
The category entry point is a vocabulary question
A large part of the journey starts at definitions and stays there, cycling through synonyms, examples and parts of speech before any product intent appears. Some of it never returns — the same word pulls in insurance, grammar and crossword questions — so education content has to actively steer back to nutrition instead of answering the language question well.
Lists are where intent finally becomes a basket
The shortlist hub is the only place the educational journey turns into named products, and from there it immediately splits by audience and by goal. Whoever owns the daily-routine list frames what the shopper buys first and what they add later.
What to do right now3
Treat store and hours queries as a retail media line item
Align with chain partners on locator accuracy, stock visibility and geo-targeted bidding around store-hour searches. This is the shortest distance to a purchase in the entire journey and it is currently decided entirely on the retailer's page.
Rebuild the explainer so it ends in a shortlist
Rework category definition and benefit pages so every one of them closes with a recommended starter set and product links, rather than with more definitions. The goal is to shorten the long language branch, not to rank for more of it.
Answer the safety question where the benefit claim lives
Put side effects, interactions and regulatory status on the same pages that make benefit claims, with verifiable sourcing. The safety branch appears early and leaves quietly, so it has to be intercepted before it becomes a reason not to buy.
Opportunities to watch3
Audience-split routine content
The daily-routine hub already forks into separate questions for women and for men. Building distinct routine guides per audience and per goal captures that split before a generic list flattens it.
Verified-brand positioning
Demand is actively screening for brands to avoid alongside brands to trust. Third-party testing and certification, made visible and earned into brand roundups, becomes a durable differentiator as scrutiny grows.
Online store trust and price as twin entries
The online branch forks into best store and cheapest place at the same moment. Owning both reassurance and value messaging on the same commerce surface keeps the journey from handing the sale to a marketplace.
